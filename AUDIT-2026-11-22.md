# Composition sweep — 22 November 2026

The owner sent a screenshot of the Overview's perspective block and wrote:
*"Problems with composition here. Check everything to make sure that we don't have anything this badly composed in our system no more."*

The screenshot shows a large empty band down the **left** of the content area: the
perspective's essay column started 184px in from the content edge, the photographs sat
beside it, and the 2px ink rule above the block still spanned the whole column. The
composition promised a full-width band and then stopped halfway across the screen.

This record covers the instrument that found it, every repair, every finding that was
answered with a reason instead of a repair, the independent review that sent the first
pass back, and the gate that keeps it from coming back.

## The instrument

`scripts/audit-composition.mjs` measures, for every routable view at 390, 1024, 1440,
1920 and 2103 px:

1. The content area is the view's own box.
2. What the reader can actually see is rasterised into a grid of 4px columns by 8px
   rows: every run of text, every graphic, every painted background, and every border
   edge as the 2px strip it actually is.
3. For each row, the stretches of columns nothing reaches.
4. A **band** is a stretch of columns that is empty in *every* row of a run of
   consecutive rows, in rows that hold content somewhere.

A margin is empty on both sides of the page; a hole is empty on one side and full on
the other. That distinction is the whole measure.

**Three bugs in the instrument itself, found and fixed before its first reading was
trusted:**

- *Text nodes were invisible.* The raster walked leaves only, so
  `<p><b>the claim</b> and its explanation</p>` reported the plain half as empty and
  invented a hole where the sentence was. It now walks every text node.
- *A border painted the whole box.* The edge painter filled the element's box for its
  top and left borders instead of the 2px strip, so any bordered box looked fully
  painted — and the perspective's band, which the rule above it crosses, disappeared.
  The band was "invisible" for exactly the reason it was a defect.
- *A vertical rule was read as a width claim.* A blockquote's 2px left rule marks the
  quote; it says nothing about how wide the page is, and the air beside a pull quote
  is the page's margin. Only horizontal rules make a width claim.

**What fails, and what does not.** A band fails when it is at least 12% of the content
width, at least 250px tall, and one of these holds:

- **(a) it is interior** — content on both sides of it in the same rows, so the page
  stops halfway across and then resumes;
- **(b) a heavy rule (2px or more) crosses it** — a rule is a claim about the width of
  the block beneath it, and a 2px ink rule that spans the content area and then
  leaves a quarter of the page empty beside it is a broken promise;
- **(c) a hairline crosses it and the content under that hairline does not reach the
  far end of it.** A hairline that divides a 1211px row and leaves 625px of that row
  empty, on every one of twenty rows, is the same broken promise one weight lighter.
  A *centred* measure inside its own frame is not a defect — the margins are the
  page's margins — so the test is whether the content is centred in the frame, not
  whether the frame is wider than the text.

Two refinements keep the third class honest, and both were learned from the review:
a rule has to **introduce** the band (sit at its top edge), because the next
section's opening rule is not a promise about the band above it; and a hairline-framed
band only fails when it **repeats** down a list — the same empty column in every
one of twenty records is a frame keeping a promise it does not keep, while a single
gap in one block is reported, answered in the record, and not failed.

A band that fails none of these is a **margin**: the view is a centred band and the
space beside it is the page's own margin. Every margin above the size threshold is
listed in the report with its size, so none is silently ignored.

## The first pass, and the review that sent it back

The first pass repaired nine places and the owner's own defect. An independent
reviewer — who did not make the changes — took the released build, captured every
view at 390/1024/1440 in scroll segments, wrote an independent pixel-level band
detector, and measured every prose line's real character count from rendered line
boxes. Its verdict was **FAIL**: the owner's screenshot was fixed, but two views
carried a stranded right half *wider* than the band the owner had rejected, one view
ran prose at ~175 characters a line and another at ~100, and two of the repairs
claimed in the first pass were inert in the shipped CSS.

Every finding in that review that held has been repaired:

1. **The News archive's records** — a 1211px row closed by a full-width hairline,
   with the text never passing x=802: 625px, 52% of the content area, empty on every
   one of the twenty records on the page. The record is two columns now — what it is on
   the left, what it claims on the right — and the analysis opens across both, centred
   (the centring was a separate fix; see "The second review" below).
2. **The Record view's curated list** — 720px empty beside every row of a 1211px row.
   The rows are three compartments now: the DOM holds **two lists of three**, not one
   list of six, so two columns left the third entry alone in a row of its own, and
   letting that entry take the row instead only moved the hole — the row was then
   1211px wide with a 359px body under it and 735px of air beside it. The house's own
   rule is that the column count divides the count, as the twin directory's five
   entries go five across and never three and two, so three entries go three across
   and every compartment holds the house's whole measure.
3. **The Record's bridge paragraph** — 100 characters a line. `ch` is the width of a
   zero, and in Jost a zero is wider than the average character: 46ch holds about 65
   characters, while 75ch held about 100. The bridge now takes the house's measure.
4. **The Research view's Insider FAQ** — 175 characters a line, in `div`s that the
   reading-measure sweep never selected. The FAQ is a measure column now.
5. **The Overview's intro** — the claimed two-column layout was inert: the grid was
   on `.overview-intro` and the lede is nested inside `.hero-text`, and a grid places
   only its own items. The grid belongs to `.hero-text` now, and the claim in the
   first pass was wrong.
6. **The Innovation view's opening row** — the row stopped 664px short of the
   content area. The video takes the slack now; it is a fixed height and crops to
   its frame, so it can widen without the composition changing shape.
7. **The Innovation view's header note at 1024** — the note sat alone on one line
   with 609px empty to its left. It reads from the left below the two-column tier.
8. **The News view's header** — `.review-head` is not a `.section-head`, so the
   two-column header never reached it. It has the same treatment now.
9. **The perspective's photograph column** — the two figures keep their own natural
   ratios, so their captions started 69px apart at 1440 and 129px apart at 1920. They
   are bottom-aligned now: the captions share a baseline and the ragged edge is the
   top of the pictures, where it belongs.

### The reading-measure gate, strengthened

The review found the measure sweep was blind in two ways, and both are fixed:

- **It assumed a character is half an em.** In Jost an average character is about
  0.45em, so every line was under-counted by about a tenth: the Record's
  100-character line measured as 89 and passed. The sweep now measures the font's
  average advance with a canvas and divides by that.
- **It did not select `div`.** The Research view's widest prose on the site — the FAQ
  at 175 characters — is a stack of `div`s and was never examined. The sweep now
  takes a `div` whose content is inline text and nothing else; a `div` holding a
  heading or a paragraph is a layout container, not a paragraph.

The ceiling was also tightened from 90 characters to **75**, the law's own ceiling.
The house's running line — 46ch, about 65 characters — sits inside the law's 60–70
band, and the three blocks that the corrected sweep caught at 92–93 characters (the
Overview's lede, the Innovation video caption, the Partners' financing notes) were
capped at the house's measure.

## The second review, and what it found

A second independent reviewer took the released build, wrote its own band detector
and line-measure harness in a temp directory, validated the detector against the
*pre-pass* build (it reproduced the owner's band exactly — 184px × 952px — and
found the classes of defect the first review had named), and then judged the
result. Its verdict on the owner's defect was that it is genuinely fixed, measured
several ways: the three compartments tile the full 1211px, the two text columns
sit at the house's 46ch measure, and the photographs' captions end on the same
baseline.

It also returned **FAIL** on three checkable things, all of which were true:

1. **The Record's curated list strands its third item.** The repair put the rows
   in two columns, and the audit record said "six divides by two". Six does
   divide by two — but the DOM holds **two lists of three**, so each list left
   its third item alone in the right-hand column with half the row empty.
   Letting the third item take the row instead does not close the hole; it
   moves it, leaving a 1211px row with a 359px body under it and 735px of air
   beside it. The list is **three compartments** now: the count divides the
   columns, so no entry strands, no compartment holds a hole, and every one of
   them keeps the house's 46ch measure.
2. **The reading-measure sweep could not see a whole view, and ran at one
   width.** Its route list contained `library`, which is not a route — the nav's
   Library link is `#data`, and `#library` falls back to the Overview, so the

3. **Two figures in this record were wrong** — the margin count above, and the
   claim that the archive's expanded analysis "opens across both", which it did
   not until the centring fix.

The review also confirmed what the first pass had repaired: the News archive's

## Findings answered with a reason, not repaired

Each is listed by the gate with its size, so none is hidden.

1. **The Essay's centred measure.** The essay is a reading page: one column of prose
   at the house's measure, centred, with its figures at their own width. The dividers
   between its sections are 1px hairlines, and the content inside them is centred, so
   the margins beside the measure are the page's margins. A measure column with
   margins either side is the house's own long-form pattern, and the law that governs
   it — "wide screens get columns or margins, never full-width paragraphs" — asks for
   exactly this.
2. **The Research view's outer band.** The consequence of finding 7: the view is a
   centred 860px band. The alternative was measured, not guessed — at full width each
   paper card holds 359px of text in a 769px box and the grid's two columns float
   430px apart.
3. **The Insights view's chart cards.** A chart inside a card 1211px wide is a chart
   with room to be a chart; the note under it is centred under it, and the
   completed-projects table is a closed band whose rules end where its columns do.
4. **The News archive's records at the narrow tier.** Below 760px of content a record
   is one column and its hairline runs the full width; the content below the rule is
   a headline and a sentence, which is what a list of records is.

## Guard

- `npm run audit:composition` — the instrument and the gate. It writes
  `artifacts/composition/composition.json` with every failure and every margin, and
  names the rule that failed a band.
- `tests/composition.spec.mjs` — the falsifier. A gate that cannot fail on the defect
  it was written for is not evidence, so the test plants the owner's defect (the
  perspective as a narrow centred pair under a full-width rule), a second one (a gulf
  inside a full-width row) and a third (a hairline framing a row whose content stops
  short), and asserts the instrument reports all three — then asserts the shipped
  Overview is clean.
- `scripts/verify-graphics.mjs` — the reading-measure sweep, now measuring the
  font's real average advance and selecting `div` prose, with the ceiling at the
  law's 75 characters.
- Both are wired into `npm run check:release`, which `npm run deploy` runs before it
  uploads anything.

## Limits

  rest by band sweep and line measurement. It did not review the light theme, the
  Thai and Chinese locales beyond three paragraphs, or the live deployed bytes.
- The 2103px viewport was added because the owner's own screen is that wide and the
  original instrument stopped at 1440px, where the perspective's band was narrower.
- A margin is judged by rule and reason, not by eye. The reasoning is written down

## 26 November — the modes nobody had measured

The owner's mandate this round was to audit the codebase against the plan of
what the system can be, kill the spatial hallucination wherever it is, and
decide what the backend needs. Three things came out of it that were not in
the two passes above.

### The gates had only ever looked at one mode

`scripts/audit-composition.mjs` measured English, dark, at five viewports,
and had always reported zero failures. The site's other two locales and its
light theme had never been swept by it — and the modes are not the same
geometry. Thai has no word spaces and Chinese sets a character per em, so
the same content breaks into different lines, and a strip of empty space that
falls at the right edge in English (a margin) can fall BETWEEN two columns
in Thai (a hole).

each mode keeps its own record in `artifacts/composition/`. The first Thai

### The reading measure could not prove its own number

An independent review of the published build reported a Research line of 77
characters where the sweep counted 73 — two over the law's ceiling of 75.
The reviewer's instrument was lost with the review, so the claim was checked
from the other end: a ground-truth probe that reads the ACTUAL rendered text
of every line of 932 prose blocks across seven views at three widths. The
worst line it found was 69 characters. A second instrument, rasterising the
rendered pixels of every view at full length and looking for empty bands
beside content, found none in any of the nineteen views.

The sweep's grouping was rebuilt anyway, because it had two ways to be wrong
and one of them was real. It grouped a line's glyphs by their exact `top`,
so a line whose glyphs sat at slightly different heights was counted in two
halves and the longer half was reported. It now groups by vertical overlap
AND requires horizontal continuity, so a multi-column note is read by its
column and not by its box. The number it reports is now the number of
characters in the line it prints, and a new control in the gate fails the
sweep if the count and the text ever disagree.

`tests/reading-measure.spec.mjs` plants the two cases: a long line carrying a
bold phrase, which must be counted as one line, and a note in a three-column
flow, which must be read by its column.

### The backend: the discovery endpoint was dead in production

`/api/ascn-news` returned 503 with an empty body on the live site. The cause
was measured rather than guessed, with a probe run from Cloudflare's own
edge: news.google.com refuses the edge's address with its "unusual
traffic" page — a 302 to google.com/sorry/index for the root, and a 503 for
the RSS endpoint — while the same feed answers normally from a workstation.
It is the publisher's policy toward the edge's address range.

No other general news index answered the same query from the edge either:
Bing's news index holds no items for the network's name, GDELT refused with
a rate limit even when paced, Yahoo answered with HTML rather than a feed,
and DuckDuckGo's news feed is gone. So the architecture was inverted to
match reality: the published snapshot is the list of record, refreshed by
`scripts/refresh-ascn-news.mjs` where the index answers, and the live check
is a bonus that says which it was. The endpoint now answers 200 with the
snapshot marked `mode: "saved"`, and gives the reason each feed gave. It
answers 503 only when the snapshot itself cannot be read, and even then with
a body that can be parsed. The client reads the mode instead of assuming a
list it received was checked live.

### Page cost is now measured

The standing law says "Measure page cost in SCREENS; cap it, only lower it."
Nothing in the repository measured it. `scripts/audit-cost.mjs` now measures
every routable view at 1440 and 390 and compares it with a cap recorded per
view in `scripts/cost-baseline.json`. A view that costs more than its cap
fails the release; a view that costs LESS than its cap also fails, because
an improvement that was not banked is a cap that has stopped being true; and
a view with no cap at all fails, because the law says cap it.

The first measurement, in screens: essay 21.7 and archive 26.3 at 390,
ascn9 1.0 at 1440. The route list is read from the document — the nav's
tabs, every view element, and the news view's own sub-panels — because a
route name that is not real falls back to the Overview and silently sweeps
the wrong view. `tests/cost.spec.mjs` plants the defect: a view padded with
air must make the gate go red, and a view that got cheaper must make it
demand a new cap.

### The static scan: what the CSS holds, and why each one is allowed

The law names the patterns to inspect: fixed heights, minimum heights,
full-width controls, fractional tracks, equal-height cards, `space-between`
and stretch alignment. Each needs a reason in the actual task, and a reason
that is not written down is a hallucination.

- **Fixed heights.** Every `min-height: 44px` in the sheet is the touch
  target floor, on a control the reader has to hit: the locale switch, the
  search field, the filter rows, the country rows, the archive's selects.
  `height: 1px` is a hairline rule. `100dvh` is the shell, which is the
  viewport and nothing else. `height: 480px` is the contacts map, an
  operating size for a map, and `20rem` is the innovation hero video, whose
  height is its frame and which crops with `object-fit: cover`. Every other
  media box carries an `aspect-ratio` instead of a height, so the picture
  decides.
- **`space-between`.** Twenty-one rules use it. Every one of them is a
  two-item row whose two items are a label and a value — the pagination's
  page label and its arrows, the news band's heading and its link, a card's
  foot with its tag, the record's filters. There is no third item in the
  middle to be stranded, so the gap is the separation between two things
  that belong apart, not a hole between two columns that are running.
- **Stretch alignment.** Two rules. The locale switch is an inline flex of
  three buttons at the 44px floor — stretched, they share one height instead
  of three. The other is the download grid's cells, which are cards of
  unequal content and are allowed to be unequal.
- **Fractional tracks.** `1fr` appears wherever the content decides: the
  record's urban table gives its label column a fixed width and the rest to
  the reading column; the archive's ledger gives the money rows their three
  fields; the insights ledger's two groups now carry two sub-columns each
  above 1600px so that four items make two rows of two and eight make four
  rows of two — both divide.

## 28 November 2026 — the trilingual round: the platform's own short words

The Thai and Chinese editions were not half translated. They were translated
where the copy was long, and left in English where it was short — and the
instrument that should have found it wanted three English words before it
would call a passage prose. A table head that said **Focus** was invisible
to it. So were a status pill that said **Ongoing**, a chart legend that said
**Completed**, a column label that said **Population**.

This round found every one of them and repaired them.

### The root cause was three, not one

**The lookup missed.** `tx()` looks the dictionary up by the English text the
renderer passes. Several call sites bind the separator to a non-breaking
space — `" · "` becomes `" · "`, so a line cannot begin with a floating
middot — and the dictionary stores the sentence as it was authored, with an
ordinary space. Every one of those lookups missed, and the page rendered
the English sentence on a Thai page. The dictionary now carries a second
index, whitespace-normalised, built only where it is safe: a normalised
string whose members disagree on what they say is left out entirely, so a
lookup can never pick the wrong reading. Normalising creates **zero** new
collisions, and the three target strings each resolve to a unique entry.

**The render path never called it.** Focus-area names, status pills, SVG
`<text>` labels, static `<th>` headers, badges, language tags — a long list
of places where the platform's own words were written into the page without
going through the dictionary. Every one is now routed through `tx()` or
carries a `data-i18n` key.

**The word count was in the wrong place.** The three-word floor belonged to
the *"no translation exists at all"* class, where its job is to tell a name
from a sentence. It was also gating the *broken render path* class, where a
four-word heading that the dictionary plainly carried was reported as
nothing at all. The floor now sits only where it earns its keep.

### The archive view had never been swept

The i18n audit's route list did not include the archive. It does now, and
the archive brings its own copy in three languages — so the question it
raises is not "is this translated" but "whose wording is this".

The archive record holds two kinds of text. A field that carries language
arms is the platform's prose, and the reader gets their own language. A
field that is a plain string — a domain, an institutional label, a figure
quoted from a document — is the wording the source issued, and it keeps that
wording in every language, because there is no second wording to give it.
The arms are not always the whole story: a journal's name carries arms, and
both arms are the English, because a journal's name is not the platform's
to translate.

So the renderer now says which is which. A field the reader is seeing in
English is marked `data-source-issued`, and the audit honours the mark.
Without it, twenty-eight citations were reported as untranslated platform
prose on the Thai and Chinese archive views, and the only way to silence
them would have been to rewrite the evidence record — which the archive's
fingerprint exists to prevent.

### The dictionary was saying one English sentence in two ways

Eight entries claimed the same English text with different readings. Three
of them were the pairs the earlier pass had left standing: *"26 ASCN cities,
10 sectors"* read as *10 สาขา* in one place and *10 ภาคส่วน* in another; a
city list in Latin in one place and transliterated in another; a China
partnership sentence worded two ways. One English sentence, one reading: the
Partners card and the Record table now say the same thing, and the sectors
read as sectors rather than branches.

The audit now **fails** on any group whose members disagree, so this cannot
come back silently.

### Six entries had a corrupt English arm

An entry whose English arm is half Thai can never answer the lookup the
renderer makes. Six were in that state, and one of them was a published
paper's title that had been given a Thai rendering — **"Evidence from
สิงคโปร์"** — which is the exact defect the October audit restored when it
put two published titles back to the publisher's wording. Two essay section
headings carried their Thai arms in the English slot. Two archive search
queries carried a query in a language they were not in, and nothing
referenced them.

The English arms are repaired, the orphans are gone, and the audit now
**fails** on any entry whose English arm carries a non-Latin letter.

### The measure, in every script

The reading-measure sweep was Latin-only, and the character ceiling is a
Latin calibration. Measured on this site at 1440: Latin 64 characters to a
line, Thai 50, Han 38 — about 27em in each script. A Chinese paragraph that
had lost its measure column reads as 68 characters, sails under the
75-character ceiling, and runs three measures wide.

The ceiling is now derived from the design's own tokens — `--measure-paper-a5`
÷ `--t-body` = 410 ÷ 13 = **31.54em** — and it is the only form of the law
that means the same thing in every script. The character ceiling stays for
Latin, where it is the law's own number.

Swept at 1440, 1024 and 390 in all three languages: the widest line is
**27.13em** in Latin, **26.81em** in Thai, **25.59em** in Chinese. All
inside the block the design allows. The sweep is now wired into the release
gate in three languages, and the falsifier plants the exact case the
character ceiling cannot see: a Chinese line that is legal in characters
and illegal in width.

### The cost of this round

No cap was raised. The trilingual round added dictionary entries, not
content: the pages carry the same sentences they carried, read in the
reader's language.

### What the gate can now fail

The i18n rule now lives in `lib/i18n-audit.mjs`, which the gate and its
falsifier both import — the test used to carry its own copy of the rule,
and a change to one could leave the other testing a rule the site no longer
had. Seven tests prove it:

- a planted English sentence on a Thai page is caught, and stops being caught
  when it is marked source-issued;
- a planted **one-word** label the dictionary carries is a broken render path;
- a sentence already partly in the reader's script is not an English leak;
- a one-word English name is not called a missing sentence;
- the shipped views carry no untranslated platform prose, in Thai and Chinese;
- the dictionary's English arms are English;
- the dictionary says one English sentence in one way.

### The "77-character line" — reproduced, and repaired

The reviewer's own census counted **39,311 individually measured rendered

The house sweep could not find them for two reasons, both in

- the selector was `"p, li, dd, blockquote, figcaption, div, span"` — **no

- and it skipped every block whose text was **shorter than 120 characters**,

strong, em, b, i, small, label, button, h1–h6, time, summary, caption, cite,
legend, output, mark`. The law was restated in `scripts/measure-law.mjs`: a
**prose** line is at most 75 characters *and* at most 31.54em (the A5 text
block, 410px, over the body size — the script-neutral form of the same
rule), and a **running** line — a title, a metadata line, a headline, a job
title — is at most 410px physical. Table cells are bound by their column;
SVG text is bound by its viewBox.

**The offenders are gone.** The library ledger was rebuilt as a card grid
whose title and metadata lines are capped at the measure, and the sweep —
English and dark, at 1440, 1024 and 390 — went from **29 failures to 0**.
The widest line in the sweep is now a table caption, which is exempt because
a table cell's measure is its column.

**The repair.** The archive head is now two columns: the title block on the
