# Release audit — 29 October 2026

Resumed MiniMax's three-file working tree (`app.js`, `ascn-news.js`, `lib/i18n.mjs`) from `ace06ae`.
It was a language pass that stopped half-built. This record supersedes any release claim it made.

## Defects found

1. **Landing page crash.** `renderOverview()` called `fill()` which `app.js` never imported. The
   Overview tab would have thrown a `ReferenceError` on first render. Imported as `fillText`
   (the file already has two local `const fill` bindings).
2. **Wrong function.** `esc(tx("Coverage gap — {pillar}:"), { pillar })` handed the slot values to
   `esc`, so a literal `{pillar}` would have been printed. Now `esc(fillText(…))`.
3. **Inert translation.** 77 strings were wrapped in `tx()`/`fill()` but the dictionary held no
   entry for any of them, so Thai and Chinese rendered English everywhere the wrapper was added.
4. **No repaint.** `ascn-news.js` never listened for `ascn:lang`, so its generated copy only changed
   on the next filter interaction after a language switch.

## Changes

95 Thai and Chinese entries added to `lib/i18n.mjs` (News archive, Overview, Research, alumni
notice, stage/source/mandate/access vocabulary, the three mandate questions). Slot markers match
across all three languages, checked by script before splicing. `ascn-news.js` now repaints on
`ascn:lang`. Prose authored in the data files (archive claims, analyses, follow-ups, knowledge-base
text) still falls back to English; that is unchanged and still disclosed by the footer.

**Guard:** `tests/i18n.unit.mjs` fails when any literal passed to `tx()`/`fillText()` in `app.js`
or `ascn-news.js` lacks a Thai or Chinese entry, or when its `{slots}` differ. Verified by mutation:
deleting one entry fails it; restoring passes. It does not catch an unimported identifier; the
browser suite's Overview render is what covers that class.

## Evidence

Unit: 70 pass, 0 fail, 1 existing external-source skip. Browser: 85 pass (a first run on the
unfixed tree was discarded: its server died mid-suite, so most failures were connection refusals,
not findings). `check:release` passed all six phases; reading sweep complete, 648 frames, 19 routes,
3 viewports. No layout, colour, type or spacing rule changed; the visual gates are unchanged and
were not re-reviewed by a second person.

## Limits

Thai and Chinese text here is authored by an agent and has had no native-reader review. Screen-reader
output on a real device is unverified. `codex/restore-readable-maps` is already an ancestor of
`main` (no unique commits); its worktree at `../ascn-map-recovery` is redundant and left in place.

# Second pass — closing the language debt

The first pass translated the code's own literals. It could not reach the prose that lives in the
data files, because that prose reaches the page as a *value* rather than a literal: `tx(row.analysis)`
carries a sentence out of `ascn-news-archive.json`, and no amount of dictionary entries makes that
sentence translate unless the dictionary holds that exact English text.

## Measured before and after

`npm run audit:graphics` counts the passages that still render in English in Thai and in Chinese.

| `ace06ae` (before any language pass) | 195 | 195 |
| `1d356e6` (code literals translated) | 162 | 162 |
| This pass | **0** | **0** |

The gate now reports **126/126 checks passed**.

## What was added

- **478 dictionary entries**, authored here, covering the reviewed news archive (claims, analyses,
  follow-ups, metric labels and units, the eight search routes), the public source library (every
  takeaway), the knowledge layer (governance, framework, ASCAP, history, partnerships, financing,
  the M&E gap, comparative position, citizen impact, document notes) and the research synthesis
  (eight themes, eight FAQ pairs, fourteen paper summaries, three capacity programmes, three
  programme networks, four insight signals).
- **Render sites wired** where prose was reaching the page untranslated: the archive's record
  fields, metric lines, review method, status and pagination; the library takeaways; the knowledge
  fields; the research themes, FAQ, bibliography and essay notes; the history moments; the ASCAP
  note; the governance model; the contacts source note.
- **`data/ascn-issued-names.json`** — a published register of names that exist in exactly one
  language in their source: the essay's published works and three annual-meeting session titles.
  The release audit reads it and strips those names from a passage before measuring it.

## Two defects found along the way

1. **A key leaked into the page.** `app.js:2254` used `data-i18n="a.eight.themes.from.the.literature"`,
   and that key was not in the dictionary — so `t()` returned the key and the Research route's
   section heading read `a.eight.themes.from.the.literature` in every language. The entry is now
   there, and the two neighbouring section labels (`ACADEMIC GEOGRAPHY`, `INSIDER PERSPECTIVE`) had
   no key at all and were never translated.
2. **A misspelled heading.** The same label read `ACADEMIC GEOOOGRAPHY` — a doubled O. It is now
   `ACADEMIC GEOGRAPHY`. The dictionary had the correct spelling all along, which is how the
   mismatch surfaced.

## The audit was changed, and why that is not weakening it

`scripts/verify-graphics.mjs` compared a whole passage to one dataset value, so a sentence that
*contained* a published name could not be judged at all — it was either wholly exempt or wholly
flagged. The test now strips every issued name from the passage and measures what the platform
actually wrote. That is stricter, not looser: prose surrounding an issued name is still measured,
and only names the site publishes can be stripped. The exemption stays earned — it is still read
from published datasets fetched from the deployed bytes, and the new register is a published
dataset like any other.

## Guards

`tests/i18n.unit.mjs` gained two checks, both mutation-tested:

- every authored prose string in the published data has a Thai and a Chinese entry, using the same
  English-prose rule the release audit applies (eight Latin words, three function words), with the
  same exemption for names a source issued;
- no two dictionary entries claim the same English text with different wording — `BY_TEXT` keeps
  the first match, so a second entry saying something else is unreachable through `tx()`.

The footer's translation note now says what is true: the translations were written by an agent and
have not had native-reader review; source headlines, document titles, organisation and project
names keep their published wording.

## Source review for the new published file

`data/ascn-issued-names.json` is new and published. It contains five names, each already public on
the site: two published works by the platform's own author, and three session titles from the ASCN
annual meetings and the Smart City World Congress. No private material, no correspondence, no
internal drafts. The public-record fingerprint (`scripts/public-records.sha256`) covers the public
archive only and is unchanged, because the archive itself is unchanged. The new file is
allowlisted in `scripts/site-assets.mjs` and is fetched by the release audit from the deployed
bytes, so the exemption it grants can only be claimed by bytes that are actually live.

# Third pass — scroll reward

The owner asked for the same measure that governs the other interfaces: every scroll must pay, and
a page that asks the reader to scroll through mostly empty space makes them uninterested in the
system. So the scroll was measured rather than judged, at 390 / 1024 / 1440, and the routes were
ranked by how much screen each word bought.

**The instrument.** The route's view is its own scroll container (`#plate`), so the number that
counts is the view's `scrollHeight` — not the document's, and not a hidden element. An earlier
draft of this measurement read the document and got 844px for every route, which is the same
mistake the audits warn about: a PASS on the wrong surface. The measure was corrected before any
conclusion was drawn from it.

**Before and after** (390px, screens of scroll):

| Route | Before | After | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| insights | 20.96 | 18.73 | −2.23 |
| projects | 3.85 | 3.05 | −0.80 |
| news/regional | 4.46 | 3.94 | −0.52 |
| history | 8.23 | 8.02 | −0.21 |

## What was changed

1. **The bar row on a phone.** At ≤520px a bar row collapsed to one column, so a label, a bar, a
   value and two sub-labels took five lines. It now keeps its three columns at phone width, which
   makes the entry two lines. The sub-label wraps inside its own column instead of running past
   it. This is the density chart on /insights and the bar lists on /projects.
2. **The country on every city.** Thirty-eight city entries each printed the country's full name —
   the same word thirty-eight times, in capitals, on a line of its own. It is now a two-letter code
   beside the city name. The full name stays in the title attribute, in the accessible name, and
   in the country filter above the wall, so nothing is lost and a line per entry is.
3. **A key that was being rendered.** The new "Joined" entry was first written as
   `tx("city.joined")` — but `tx()` looks a string up by its English text, not by its key, so the
   key landed in the accessible name. It is fixed, and `tests/i18n.unit.mjs` now fails on any
   wrapped literal that renders a dictionary key. That is the same class as the Research route's
   leaked heading, so it is now caught by a gate rather than by reading.

## What was measured and deliberately not changed

The remaining thin routes are thin because they are text-light, not because they are empty: /partners
is three programmes with substantial paragraphs, /history is photographs with their captions and
wording, /cities is a map and a wall of thirty-eight entries. The screenshots show no empty space
to reclaim, and cutting their content would cost evidence rather than air. The three longest
routes at 390px — /essay 22.98 screens at 252 words per screen, /data 21.27 at 203, /research 11.91
at 227 — are long because they are long documents, and they are packed.

The MSH spatial audit reports **0 findings** across 22 views, chrome cost 2103px (9.65% of the
viewport) and rail 8.26%, before and after this pass. No layout, colour, type or spacing rule
changed for the desktop or tablet widths.
