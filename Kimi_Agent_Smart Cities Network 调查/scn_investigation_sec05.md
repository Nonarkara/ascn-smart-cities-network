## 5. SCN's Asia-Pacific Engagements

SCN's claimed Asia-Pacific footprint anchors its credibility. The website lists thirteen "Partner Cities" spanning Singapore, Bangkok, Jakarta, Kuala Lumpur, Manila, Iligan, Santa Rosa, Cauayan City, and Phnom Penh[^1017^]. Its chairman, Kok Chin Tay, holds titles suggesting deep government integration: "Smart City Advisor and Capacity Builder for the Philippines DOST," "Consulting Expert for the Phnom Penh Smart and Sustainable City Strategic Roadmap 2035," and "UNESCAP Smart City Consultant" for Cambodia[^1003^][^1001^][^565^].

A systematic review against primary government documents and independent sources reveals a stark divergence. The Philippines engagements are genuine but far more limited than marketing suggests. The Cambodian claims lack any government verification. And critically, SCN has no formal relationship with Singapore's Smart Nation initiative despite being headquartered there. The pattern is not of a well-connected implementer, but of an organization whose verifiable government touchpoints are concentrated in developing countries where due diligence capacity is weaker.

### 5.1 Philippines — The Most Documented Engagement

The Philippines represents SCN's most thoroughly documented government engagement. Multiple independent sources confirm MOUs, workshops, and advisory relationships with Philippine city governments and the Department of Science and Technology. However, the substance of these engagements — what was actually delivered — falls well short of what SCN's credentials imply.

#### 5.1.1 DOST STI Ambassador (December 2021) — Verified but Limited Outcomes

Kok Chin Tay's speaker biographies consistently list his appointment as an "STI Ambassador" by the Philippines Department of Science and Technology in December 2021, described as a "prime mover" for the country's smart city initiatives[^565^][^572^]. The title appears on official Philippine government sources — the Philippine News Agency and DOST event listings both refer to him as "Dr. Kok Chin Tay," lending the claim government imprimatur[^656^].

The "STI Ambassador" programme is a real DOST initiative to enlist industry leaders to promote science and technology across Philippine cities. Tay's appointment appears in DOST event coverage for Regional Science, Technology, and Innovation Week in CALABARZON[^656^]. However, no primary DOST press release explicitly confirming the specific title and date was located.

What is clear is that the title has produced no documented project outcomes. Three years after the claimed appointment, no SCN-delivered project appears in DOST records. The department's own Smart and Sustainable Cities Framework was developed with the Department of Information and Communications Technology, not SCN[^1030^]. DOST's iSTART programme initiatives involve department officials directly, with no SCN involvement[^1026^]. The title functioned primarily as credentials enhancement — a government-affiliated designation that opened doors without requiring measurable deliverables.

#### 5.1.2 Cauayan City MOU (January 2019) — Signed but Implementation by Third Parties

The Cauayan City engagement is SCN's most substantiated government partnership. In January 2019, SCN signed a memorandum of understanding with Cauayan City (population ~160,000, Isabela Province), the Department of Science and Technology, Isabela State University, and the Smart Cities Council for the "Smarter and Sustainable Cities in the Philippines" project[^613^][^615^]. Multiple independent sources confirm this MOU: icleiseas.org, Cauayan City's own publications, and the League of Cities of the Philippines all document the signing and subsequent workshops[^613^][^615^][^660^].

The partnership produced genuine activity. A "First Smarter Cities Readiness Challenge Workshop" was held involving Cauayan City, DOST Regional Office II, Isabela State University, the League of Cities of the Philippines, and Kok Chin Tay[^660^]. The engagement culminated in a digital twin project — a 3D virtual model of Cauayan City for disaster resilience — which was used to assess PHP 600 million (~USD 12.5 million) in typhoon damage, identify 7,724 affected families, and coordinate distribution of 36,824 relief packs[^1187^].

But the digital twin was not delivered by SCN. Technical implementation was performed by Graffiquo, a Singapore-based geospatial provider, "introduced to the city through a mutual Smart Cities Network contact"[^1185^]. Funding came from WeGO (World Smart Sustainable Cities Organization), which sponsored approximately USD 92,000 in services[^1183^]. The project received an AWS "Global GameChanger" award — an Amazon marketing programme, not government recognition[^568^].

SCN's role was facilitator and connector — valuable, but fundamentally different from implementation. Tay promoted Cauayan City on global platforms and SCN's network bridged the city to Graffiquo[^1039^]. Yet SCN markets this as one of its "13 Key Projects"[^1017^], obscuring that a third-party vendor performed the actual work, funded by an external organisation.

#### 5.1.3 Santa Rosa City and Iligan City MOUs (2023) — No Outcomes After 18+ Months

Following the Cauayan model, SCN signed additional MOUs with Santa Rosa City (October 6, 2023) and Iligan City (August 15, 2023), both framed as "Capacity Building for Smart and Sustainable Cities" partnerships[^1069^]. These agreements expanded SCN's Philippine city portfolio and provided further credentials for conference bios and marketing materials.

Eighteen months later, no specific project outcomes, deliverables, or implementation details from either partnership have materialised in any publicly accessible source. No digital twins, no readiness workshops documented by independent observers, no government reports citing SCN involvement — only the MOUs themselves, which SCN lists as achievements[^1069^]. The pattern established at Cauayan City, where at minimum a third-party vendor delivered tangible results, has not been replicated. These agreements appear to exist as paper partnerships: signed, announced, and then dormant.

### 5.2 Cambodia — Unverified Claims

If the Philippines engagement is SCN's most documented, Cambodia is its most inflated. Kok Chin Tay claims three distinct Cambodian credentials: Visiting Professor at the National University of Management (NUM), Consulting Expert for Phnom Penh's Smart City Strategic Roadmap, and UN ESCAP consultant for Cambodia's National Smart City Policy Guidelines[^567^][^565^]. None of these claims withstand scrutiny against primary Cambodian government or UN documents.

#### 5.2.1 NUM Cambodia "Visiting Professor" — Listed but Limited

The National University of Management's Master of Science in Digital Economy page does list "Professor Kok Chin (KC) Tay" as providing lectures on "Components of Smart City Infrastructure"[^576^]. His own bio states he has been "actively involved in developing courses regarding smart city management planning at Faculty of Digital Economy"[^576^].

This represents the most verifiable of the Cambodian claims. However, the position appears to be a course contributor or guest lecturer arrangement rather than a formal visiting professorship with academic appointment, peer review obligations, or institutional governance role. Cambodian universities frequently host foreign practitioners for short-term lecture series, and the "Professor" designation in this context reflects courtesy titling rather than academic rank. The claim is real but modest — a teaching contribution inflated into an academic appointment in SCN's marketing.

#### 5.2.2 Phnom Penh — No Cambodian Government Documents

The most consequential Cambodian claim is also the most unsupported. Tay lists himself as "Consulting Expert for the Implementation Masterplan 2035 for the Phnom Penh Smart and Sustainable City Strategic Roadmap"[^567^]. The roadmap itself is a genuine government document, adopted in 2021 with the roadmap period extending to 2035[^1167^].

However, no Cambodian government document credits either Tay or SCN with its development. A January 2022 article from Construction-Property.com reporting the roadmap's unveiling notes cooperation with "AIMF, EU, Japan MLIT, JICA, ADB, among others" — with no mention of SCN or Kok Chin Tay[^670^]. The October 2021 ASEAN-Japan Smart Cities High Level Meeting presentation by Phnom Penh Capital Hall lists the roadmap as a Cambodian government initiative supported by international development partners; SCN is absent[^676^]. An April 2024 AIMF terms of reference for the Phnom Penh Smart City Hub calls for consultants with "15 years' experience in Cambodia and South-East Asia" — Tay is not named[^579^].

What does exist is a November 2021 presentation by Tay himself titled "Harnessing 4IR technologies for sustainable and smart cities," in which he describes Phnom Penh work as a proposal and collaboration concept, not a delivered project[^568^]. Slides outline goals to "Position Phnom Penh as a model Smart & Sustainable Capital City" and "Collaborate with the World Bank, Asian Development Bank, UN Agencies" — aspirational objectives, not completed work[^568^]. The SCN website lists Phnom Penh as one of "13 Partner Cities"[^708^], but no formal partnership documentation has been located.

#### 5.2.3 UN ESCAP — Only in Self-Published Bios

The third Cambodian claim — that Tay served as "UNESCAP Smart City Consultant to help develop the National Smart City Policy Guidelines for Cambodia"[^565^] — appears exclusively in self-published speaker bios and SCN marketing materials. Multiple speaker profiles (Asia Institute of Mentoring, PIFA, and others) repeat the claim[^565^][^572^].

No verifiable UN ESCAP document crediting Tay was located. A December 2025 preprint paper on Cambodian sustainable urban development discusses ASEAN Smart Cities Network implementation but does not mention Tay or SCN[^573^]. A UN ESCAP stakeholder consultation workshop presentation on "Cambodia's National Smart City Strategy" was referenced in search results but could not be fully accessed for verification[^599^]. The claim exists entirely outside official UN documentation — a credential that has propagated through the conference circuit without institutional verification.

### 5.3 The Developing Country Targeting Pattern

The geographic distribution of SCN's verifiable government engagements reveals a pattern inconsistent with a Singapore-based organisation claiming regional smart city expertise.

#### 5.3.1 Engagements Concentrated in Philippines and Cambodia — Not Singapore Where Headquartered

SCN is registered in Singapore (UEN 201827719R, incorporated August 2018)[^732^][^733^]. Its stated address is 531A Upper Cross Street, #04-98 Hong Lim Complex — a commercial building shared by over 100 companies, suggesting a virtual office arrangement rather than a substantial physical presence[^655^]. Yet Singapore's own smart city initiative, Smart Nation, led by the Smart Nation and Digital Government Office (SNDGO) and GovTech, has no documented formal relationship with SCN[^614^][^616^][^617^].

This absence is striking. Singapore is one of the world's most advanced smart city implementers, with comprehensive government digitalisation, national digital identity systems, and extensive smart infrastructure. An organisation genuinely embedded in Singapore's smart city ecosystem would reasonably be expected to have some connection to Smart Nation. SCN does not.

Instead, SCN's verifiable government touchpoints are concentrated in the Philippines and Cambodia — developing countries with weaker institutional capacity to verify foreign credentials. The Philippines engagement, while real, relied partly on a title ("Dr.") that Tay does not hold[^656^]. The Cambodia claims are entirely unsubstantiated by government documents. Both countries operate in institutional environments where foreign-sounding affiliations — UN partnerships, Singapore-registered entities, professorial titles — carry significant weight and may not be rigorously verified.

#### 5.3.2 Singapore's Smart Nation Office Has No Formal Relationship with SCN

Kok Chin Tay's genuine Singapore government connection dates to 2012, when he "project managed the Smarter Cities Challenge in collaboration with the Singapore government" — but this was under IBM, before SCN existed[^567^][^572^]. The IBM Smarter Cities Challenge for Jurong Lake District was a legitimate, well-documented engagement. It provides Tay with a genuine credential, but one that predates and is separate from SCN.

Since SCN's incorporation in 2018, no evidence of any formal engagement with SNDGO, GovTech, the Ministry of National Development, or any Singapore government agency has been located. Singapore's approach to smart city governance emphasises rigorous vendor accreditation, transparent procurement through the GeBIZ system, and direct government-led implementation. SCN has not appeared in any of these channels. The organisation is, in effect, a Singapore-registered entity without a Singapore government relationship.

#### 5.3.3 Pattern: Developed-Country Registration for Credibility, Developing-Country Business

The pattern that emerges is a familiar one in the international consulting industry. SCN leverages its Singapore registration — a jurisdiction associated with regulatory rigour, financial transparency, and good governance — to establish credibility with officials in developing countries who may not distinguish between registration location and operational substance. The Singapore address, the UN-Habitat MOU, the ERIA workgroup participation, and the various conference titles create a credentials package that appears impressive in contexts where verification resources are limited.

Simultaneously, SCN avoids engagement in Singapore itself, where its lack of government relationships, absence of delivered projects, one-person operational structure, and non-charity status would be more readily apparent to local officials and potential partners. The organisation is structurally configured for developing-country engagement: it can present Singapore-registered credentials, claim UN affiliations, and offer advisory titles that sound authoritative without the scrutiny that would accompany similar claims in a developed-country context.

This targeting pattern does not constitute evidence of illegality. The Philippines MOUs are real. The NUM teaching role is verified. The Cauayan City connection facilitated a tangible outcome, even if SCN did not deliver it directly. But the concentration of engagement in countries with weaker due diligence capacity, combined with zero engagement in SCN's home jurisdiction, raises questions about whether the business model depends partly on information asymmetry — the ability to present credentials that appear more substantial than independent verification would confirm.

The user's original concern about deals with authoritarian figures, while directed at SCC, finds an echo here. Cambodia's government operates with constrained civil society scrutiny, and foreign consultants may operate with limited transparency. The claims Tay makes about Cambodian advisory roles cannot be verified because the institutional infrastructure to verify them is either absent or inaccessible. This opacity is the enabling condition for credentials that exist primarily in self-published bios rather than government records.
