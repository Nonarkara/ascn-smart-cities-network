## 3. Smart Cities Network — What It Actually Is

On paper, Smart Cities Network Ltd is a legitimate Singapore entity with a valid Unique Entity Number, an appropriate not-for-profit corporate structure, and a seven-year operating history. [^1015^] But peel back the registration documents and what emerges is not an organization in any conventional sense — it is a single individual, a rented mailbox, and a collection of unverified partnership claims sustained by the structural invisibility that Singapore corporate law affords to non-charitable companies.

This chapter examines SCN's legal foundations, governance vacuum, and financial opacity to establish what the organization actually is.

### 3.1 Legal Status

#### 3.1.1 Registered as "SMART CITIES NETWORK LTD." (UEN: 201827719R) — Company Limited by Guarantee, incorporated 14 August 2018

SCN is registered with Singapore's Accounting and Corporate Regulatory Authority (ACRA) as SMART CITIES NETWORK LTD, UEN 201827719R, incorporated 14 August 2018 as a Public Company Limited by Guarantee, with registered address at 531A Upper Cross Street, #04-98 Hong Lim Complex, Singapore 051531. [^1015^] [^1000^]

The Company Limited by Guarantee (CLG) structure is Singapore's standard vehicle for not-for-profit organizations. It has no share capital or shareholders; members act as guarantors who agree to pay a nominal sum if the company dissolves. [^1057^] The structure is common and legitimate — used by trade associations, religious bodies, and genuine charities.

What is remarkable is the timing. SCN was incorporated exactly four months after the official ASEAN Smart Cities Network (ASCN) was established at the 32nd ASEAN Summit on 28 April 2018. [^1012^] "Smart Cities Network" is "ASEAN Smart Cities Network" with one word removed. SCN's website contains no disclaimer clarifying it is not the intergovernmental ASCN. [^1000^] The CLG structure provides legal legitimacy; the name harvests credibility from an official ASEAN initiative.

#### 3.1.2 NOT a registered charity — pays 17% corporate tax

SCN is not registered as a charity under Singapore's Charities Act. A search of the Singapore Charity Portal returns zero results for "Smart Cities Network." [^1072^] [^1446^] Charity registration requires demonstration of exclusively charitable purposes and registration with the Commissioner of Charities — a standard SCN has not met. [^1064^] [^1108^]

The consequences are concrete:

- **No automatic income tax exemption** under section 13(1)(zm) of the Income Tax Act [^1067^]
- **No property tax exemption** on premises used for charitable purposes
- **Cannot issue tax-deductible receipts** for donations (no IPC status)
- **Not subject** to the Charity Council's Code of Governance

SCN pays Singapore's standard corporate income tax rate of 17% on chargeable income. [^1396^] [^1397^] Whatever "not-for-profit" means to SCN, it does not mean the tax-exempt status that Singapore law associates with charitable activity.

#### 3.1.3 "Not-for-profit" is a self-descriptor with no legal basis

The term "not-for-profit" appears throughout SCN's website, press releases, and Chairman Kok Chin Tay's biographies. [^1159^] [^1099^] But in Singapore corporate law, "not-for-profit" has no independent legal meaning. It is a self-descriptor, not a regulated category.

A CLG cannot distribute profits to members, which creates a not-for-profit *structure*. But without charity registration, no regulator reviews whether SCN's activities serve charitable purposes, whether directors fulfill fiduciary duties, or whether resources are deployed for public benefit. The label becomes a statement of aspiration — one SCN deploys to solicit partnerships without submitting to the accountability mechanisms that legitimate Singapore nonprofits accept.

#### 3.1.4 Virtual office at Hong Lim Complex (100+ companies share the address)

SCN's registered address at 531A Upper Cross Street, #04-98 Hong Lim Complex is a virtual office in a commercial building shared by more than 100 companies. [^1053^] Multiple corporate service providers list the exact same unit as their registered address offering. [^1058^] [^1054^]

The arrangement is legal and common. But it means SCN almost certainly maintains no physical office, no staff to visit, and no operational headquarters. The "office" is a mailbox in a shared business center — consistent with a one-person operation rather than the regional network SCN claims to be. [^1000^]

### 3.2 Governance Reality

#### 3.2.1 KC Tay is sole identifiable person — no board, no staff, no employees named

After exhaustive searches across SCN's website, press releases, conference programs, academic papers, and government documents, exactly one person is identified with SCN in any leadership capacity: Kok Chin Tay, the "Founding Chairman." [^572^] [^1003^] [^1001^]

No other individual has ever been named as a board member, director, employee, volunteer, or officer in any public source across seven years. The SCN "Contact Us" page lists a single email address and one mobile phone number. [^1000^] No departmental contacts. No media inquiries line. No administrative support.

This is the profile of a personal consulting vehicle, not a seven-year-old organization claiming 13 partner cities across five ASEAN nations. [^1159^]

#### 3.2.2 "Executive Council" claimed but zero members named

SCN's website "Structure" page describes a hierarchy topped by an "Executive Council" — the "strategic leadership and governance body overseeing the network's mission and global alignment." [^527^] Below it sit claimed "Regional Chapters," "Working Groups" (Mobility, Sustainability, Cyber Security), a "Youth Network (SCYN)" at two Singapore universities, and a "Member Community." [^1403^]

Not a single Executive Council member is named. No terms of reference, meeting schedules, or minutes are published. Regional Chapters have no listed locations or leaders. Working Groups have no chairs. The Youth Network has no documented activities. [^527^]

This is a hollow governance structure — organizational chart labels without substance. The Executive Council exists as a conceptual placeholder, giving SCN the appearance of collective decision-making while all authority rests with one person.

#### 3.2.3 Uses SCC email for SCN business

Multiple official documents list KC Tay's contact as **KC.TAY@SmartCitiesCouncil.com**, not SCN's own smartcitiesnetwork.org domain. [^1039^] [^1085^] Tay has stated: "My name is Kok Chin (KC) Tay, Chairman of the Smart Cities Network and the ASEAN Exec Director of US-based Smart Cities Council." [^1060^]

This dual role creates significant conflict of interest: SCN claims to be an independent not-for-profit network, while SCC is a global commercial ecosystem with paying members. By directing SCN communications through SCC infrastructure, Tay blurs the organizational boundary. There is no published disclosure of this dual role, no recusal policy, and no management of the conflicts that arise when one person directs two similarly named organizations with different commercial incentives. [^1034^]

Whether SCN holds board meetings, has conflict-of-interest policies, or allows member participation in governance — all unknown. ### 3.3 Financial Opacity

#### 3.3.1 Legally EXEMPT from filing financial statements with ACRA

The structural feature enabling SCN's financial invisibility is its legal status. As a Public Company Limited by Guarantee, SCN is **exempt from filing financial statements with ACRA**. [^1336^] [^1339^] [^1342^]

CLGs must still prepare audited financial statements annually and hold an Annual General Meeting. [^1335^] [^1337^] But those statements — the document revealing revenue, expenditures, assets, and director remuneration — are presented only to members at the AGM. They are not uploaded to ACRA's public database. [^1338^]

> "CLGs are exempt from filing financial statements with ACRA. However, you still need to prepare them and have them approved by the members at the AGM." [^1338^]

SCN's financial statements could exist on paper but remain entirely inaccessible to the public, prospective partners, donors, and government officials who engage SCN as an advisor. The exemption is standard Singapore corporate law — not a loophole. But combined with SCN's non-charity status, which eliminates Charity Portal disclosure requirements, it produces near-total financial opacity.

#### 3.3.2 No annual reports, no audited financials, no budget disclosures

After seven years, SCN has published no annual report, no financial statements, no budget, and no expenditure breakdown. [^1017^] Its website claims "7+ Years of Impact" and "13 Partner Cities, 5 ASEAN Nations, 13 Key Projects," but provides no financial data. [^1017^]

The contrast with legitimate Singapore nonprofits is stark. Registered charities must submit audited financial statements to the Charity Portal for public viewing. [^1363^] IPCs must file donation returns with IRAS. Charities with income above S$500,000 must publish online financial summaries. [^1363^] SCN is subject to none of these requirements and has disclosed nothing voluntarily.

ZoomInfo estimates SCN's revenue at "<$5 million" with 11–50 employees. [^1319^] Both figures are algorithmic estimates with low reliability. The employee count is almost certainly inflated given no named staff exist. No primary financial source confirms any revenue figure.

#### 3.3.3 Membership pricing not published

SCN describes itself as "a not-for-profit individual membership-based organisation, whose members are professionals with expertise in a wide variety of themes essential to smart city development." [^1159^] But it publishes no membership fees, pricing tiers, or benefit structures.

The "Become a Member" link redirects to a Google Form for contact information submission. [^1412^] No pricing, no benefits description, no indication whether membership is free, invitation-only, or fee-based. By contrast, the Smart Cities Council — where KC Tay concurrently serves as Executive Director (ASEAN) — publishes a detailed membership matrix with tiered rates. [^12^] SCN publishes nothing.

This opacity matters because any membership fees represent revenue flowing to an organization with no published financial controls, no independent board oversight, and no obligation to account for how funds are spent.

The comparison reveals a fundamental mismatch. SCN presents itself as a regional network operating alongside ASCN, Smart Nation, and C40. But on every measure of accountability — board composition, financial transparency, governance documentation, leadership visibility, and regulatory oversight — SCN operates in a vacuum. It is not a charity, so no Charity Council obligations apply. It is not a society, so no Registry of Societies requirements bind it. It is a CLG, so its financial statements are prepared but never shared. The result is an entity that claims nonprofit legitimacy while submitting to none of its disciplines.

This is not illegal. It is by design — a design that allows one person to operate with the appearance of collective authority and the reality of solitary control, answerable to no regulator, no members, and no public for how it spends money or what it actually delivers.
