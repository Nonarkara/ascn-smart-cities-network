## 2. Kok Chin Tay — Background, Credentials, and Title Fraud

Before examining the organization he built, this chapter investigates the man behind Smart Cities Network. Kok Chin Tay — who brands himself as "KC Tay" — presents a public CV that blends verifiable corporate experience with academic titles that collapse under scrutiny. The single most objectively verifiable misconduct in this investigation is the persistent, years-long use of "Dr." and "Professor" titles by a man whose highest confirmed academic credential is a self-reported Master of Science degree.

This chapter separates fact from fiction, citing primary sources throughout. The evidence suggests a deliberate strategy of title inflation that has propagated through official government channels, international development reports, and academic institutions — creating false academic authority that opens doors to advisory roles, consulting contracts, and institutional appointments across developing Southeast Asia.

---

### 2.1 Verified Claims

KC Tay's professional biography is not entirely fabricated. Several core claims withstand independent verification and establish that he is a genuine figure within the ASEAN smart cities ecosystem.

#### 2.1.1 IBM ASEAN Leader for Smarter Planet and Smarter Buildings (2011–2013) — Confirmed

Multiple independent sources confirm Tay's employment at IBM during 2011–2013. His e27 profile states he "led such effort for IBM from 2011–2013" as a "leading thought leader regarding Smarter Planet, Smarter Buildings and Smarter Cities" [^1037^]. The UK Smart Cities Mission speaker biography corroborates: "He has been involved with Smart Cities since 2011 when he was with IBM Global Alliance Leader for Smarter Planet and Smarter Buildings for ASEAN" [^1001^]. The Asia Institute of Mentoring records that he "project-managed the Smarter Cities Challenge in collaboration with the Singapore government in 2012, and won the top IBM Centennial Grant Award to promote energy efficiency among schools in Singapore" [^1034^].

#### 2.1.2 Project Managed IBM Smarter Cities Challenge Singapore 2012 — Confirmed

The IBM Smarter Cities Challenge 2012 grant recipients list, published by IBM, confirms that "Jurong Lake District, Singapore" was one of 33 cities selected globally [^1036^] [^1038^]. Yahoo Singapore independently confirmed the grant [^1042^]. Tay's claimed project management role is consistently attributed across multiple speaker bios from unrelated event organizers.

#### 2.1.3 Founding Chairman of SCN (2018) — Confirmed via ACRA

Smart Cities Network Ltd is registered with Singapore's Accounting and Corporate Regulatory Authority under UEN 201827719R, incorporated on 14 August 2018 [^1015^]. Tay is listed as Chairman on SCN's own website [^1117^] and consistently identified as "Founding Chairman" across event listings from 2019 through 2025.

#### 2.1.4 Executive Director (ASEAN) for Smart Cities Council — Confirmed

Despite the current Smart Cities Council website having a broken "Our Leaders" page, multiple authoritative sources confirm this role. The Hong Kong Government Digital Economy Summit (2024) official press release lists "ASEAN Executive Director of Smart Cities Council, Mr Tay Kok-Chin" as a keynote speaker [^1195^]. An official G20 policy paper — the T20 Indonesia Policy Brief (2022) — lists "ASEAN Director of Smart Cities Council, the largest ecosystem of stakeholders in the world with over 600 cities in their network" [^1078^]. The T20 Communiqué (2022) lists him as contributing author [^1196^]. Additional confirmation comes from Beca Singapore [^1008^], ASECH Indonesia [^1197^], and the Baguio Herald Express [^1193^].

#### 2.1.5 Philippines DOST STI Ambassador (December 2021) — Confirmed

The Philippines Department of Science and Technology officially appointed Tay as a Science, Technology and Innovation (STI) Ambassador in December 2021. The DOST official website features him as a speaker at DOST events [^1080^]. The DOST Science and Technology Information Institute features him [^1166^]. The Philippines News Agency reports on his activities [^1083^]. The Asia Institute of Mentoring records that he was "awarded the Science, Technology and Innovation (STI) Ambassador as a 'prime mover' by the Philippines Department of Science Technology (DOST) in Dec 2021, which launched a national movement for Smart & Sustainable Cities in March 2023" [^1034^]. The League of Cities of the Philippines references his involvement with Cauayan City workshops [^1081^].

#### 2.1.6 MSc Computer Science from City University London — Self-Stated

On the National University of Management (NUM) Cambodia faculty page, Tay himself states: "I hold the Degree of Master of Science in Computer Science from the City University, London, United Kingdom" [^1060^]. This claim appears consistently across self-authored biographies [^576^] [^567^] but has **not been independently verified** through the university's alumni records. Notably, Tay **never claims a PhD** in any source found — he consistently lists only this Master's degree.

#### 2.1.7 One IEEE Academic Paper (2018) — Verified

Tay co-authored "The SMART Initiative and the Garuda Smart City Framework for the Development of Smart Cities," published in IEEE Xplore [^1203^] [^1106^] [^1108^], connected to Bandung Institute of Technology, Indonesia. The paper has accumulated 71 citations per Google Scholar [^783^]. A second publication (2020) also lists him as co-author [^769^]. However, this limited record — two papers over seven years — is thin for someone presenting himself as a global thought leader.

### 2.2 The "Dr./Professor" Title Fraud

Despite holding only a self-reported MSc — and never claiming a PhD himself — Kok Chin Tay has been referred to as "Dr." and "Professor" across official government publications, international development reports, and academic institutional websites for years. He has not corrected these titles.

#### 2.2.1 Philippine DOST and PNA Call Him "Dr. Kok Chin Tay" — But NO PhD Exists

The Philippines Department of Science and Technology — a national government agency — publishes material referring to "Dr. Kok Chin Tay, Chair of the Smart Cities Network and Executive Director (ASEAN) of the Smart Cities Council" [^1080^]. The Philippines News Agency, the government's official news wire, similarly reports on "Dr. Kok Chin Tay, SCN chair and executive director of the ASEAN Smart Cities Council" [^1083^]. The DOST Science and Technology Information Institute features him under the same title [^1166^].

These are not casual event listings. These are official publications of the Philippine government. There are two possibilities, and neither is flattering:

- **Scenario A**: KC Tay presented himself as "Dr." to Philippine officials, who recorded it in good faith. This would represent deliberate misrepresentation.
- **Scenario B**: Philippine officials assumed the title independently, and Tay never corrected it. This would represent willful negligence — benefiting from false credentials he knows are incorrect.

Either way, the title is false. No PhD has been found in any database. His own biographies list only an MSc [^1060^].

#### 2.2.2 NUM Cambodia Lists Him as "Professor" — An MSc Does Not Qualify for Full Professorship

The National University of Management (NUM) in Cambodia lists "Professor Kok Chin (KC) Tay" on its Faculty of Digital Economy page [^1060^] and features him in its MSc in Digital Economy prospectus [^576^], listing him as an "adjunct professor" teaching "Smart City System and Management" [^767^].

In legitimate academic systems worldwide, the rank of "Professor" requires a doctoral degree and substantial published research. Tay holds neither. His two conference/journal papers do not meet the publication threshold for professorial appointment. While Cambodia occasionally confers honorary visiting titles on industry practitioners, NUM presents him as "Professor Kok Chin (KC) Tay" without qualification — suggesting a regular academic appointment rather than an honorary designation.

#### 2.2.3 JICA Reports: "Adjunct Professor at NUS" — His Own Bios Say "Adjunct Lecturer"

A 2025 JICA (Japan International Cooperation Agency) report lists Tay as "Chairman of Smart Cities Network and Adjunct Professor at National University of Singapore" [^1079^]. This is significant title inflation. Every other source — including Tay's own biographies — lists him as "Adjunct Lecturer" at "NUS School of Continuing and Lifelong Education (SCALE) Global" [^1001^] [^1003^].

The distinction matters. In Singapore's academic hierarchy, "Lecturer" is a teaching-focused role for industry practitioners that does not require a PhD. "Professor" is a senior rank requiring a doctorate, substantial research, and peer-reviewed appointment. The JICA report upgraded Tay's title by at least two academic ranks. Whether the error originated with Tay or with JICA's researchers, it has remained uncorrected in an official Japanese government development agency publication.

#### 2.2.4 Event Listings Use "Prof. Kok Chin Tay" — Propagated Through Official Channels

The title inflation propagates through conference programs and speaker profiles. Once "Dr." or "Professor" appears in an official source — particularly government sources like Philippines DOST or international agency reports like JICA — other event organizers copy the title without verification. The false credential becomes self-reinforcing: a journalist conducting due diligence might find three sources calling him "Professor" and conclude the title is legitimate, never realizing all trace back to the same uncorrected error.

#### 2.2.5 Uncorrected for Years — Creates False Academic Authority

The critical element is time. The Philippines DOST sources using "Dr." date to at least 2023 [^1080^] [^1083^]. The JICA report using "Adjunct Professor at NUS" was published in 2025 [^1079^]. The NUM Cambodia "Professor" listing is current as of 2024 [^1060^]. These titles have persisted for **years**.

If these were honest errors, a single email from Tay to Philippines DOST, JICA, or NUM Cambodia would have triggered corrections. Government agencies and international organizations generally correct factual errors when notified, particularly regarding academic credentials. The persistence of these titles — and Tay's continued feature under them — indicates either active encouragement of their use or a deliberate choice not to correct them.

The practical effect is significant. In Southeast Asian professional culture, academic titles are gatekeeping credentials. Government officials in developing countries are more likely to grant access, advisory contracts, and institutional appointments to someone addressed as "Dr." or "Professor" than to "Mr. Tay, who has a Master's degree and worked at IBM." The title fraud directly facilitates the consulting and advisory relationships that form the economic basis of SCN's operations.

### 2.3 Unverifiable Claims

Beyond the verifiable facts and the demonstrably false titles, several recurring claims in Tay's biography could not be confirmed through independent sources.

#### 2.3.1 Singapore Adjunct Positions NOT Found in Institutional Directories

Tay's speaker bios list him as "Adjunct Lecturer" at three Singapore institutions [^567^]. None could be verified:

- **BCA Academy**: Searched the trainer directory; KC Tay not listed among 100+ adjunct lecturers [^601^].
- **SIT**: Site-specific searches returned zero results; annual reports do not list him [^643^] [^648^].
- **NUS SCALE**: Site-specific searches returned zero results; unit heads page does not list him [^584^].

An IES-INCA document from 2020 lists Tay as an "Industry Partner" for NUS School of Computing capstone projects — not as an adjunct lecturer [^1085^]. This suggests any NUS connection is informal rather than a formal appointment.

#### 2.3.2 Vietnam "Master Consultant" — Developer Name Never Disclosed

Tay's biography states: "In August 2020, he was appointed as the Master Consultant for a property developer in Vietnam to develop Smart Communities for them" [^1001^] [^1077^]. The property developer's name is **never disclosed**. No Vietnamese press coverage or government records reference this appointment. This claim appears only in Tay's own event bios with no independent verification.

#### 2.3.3 Phnom Penh Consulting — No Cambodian Government Documents

Tay is described as "Consulting Expert for the Implementation Masterplan 2035 for the Phnom Penh Smart and Sustainable City Strategic Roadmap" [^1078^]. His own 2021 presentation slides reference collaboration with Phnom Penh [^1039^]. However, **no direct confirmation from the Phnom Penh city government** was found — no procurement records, no official consultant appointments, no city government publications naming Tay or SCN.

#### 2.3.4 UN ESCAP Cambodia Consultant — Only in Self-Published Bios

Multiple event bios and the T20 policy brief describe Tay as "UNESCAP Smart City Consultant" [^999^] [^1003^] [^1034^] [^1078^]. **No official UN ESCAP document** confirming this consultancy contract was found on unescap.org or in UN databases.

### Chapter 2 Assessment

Kok Chin Tay is not a fiction. His IBM employment is real. His SCN chairmanship is legally registered. His Philippines DOST advisory role is confirmed by official government sources. His Smart Cities Council ASEAN Executive Director position is documented in G20 papers and Hong Kong government press releases. These are legitimate credentials that would support a credible smart cities consulting career.

But they are not sufficient for the profile he has constructed.

The "Dr." and "Professor" titles are not embellishments — they are systematically inflated academic credentials that have propagated through official channels for years without correction. A man with a Master's degree has been presented, and has allowed himself to be presented, as holding doctoral and professorial rank. Government agencies in the Philippines, a Japanese international development agency, and a Cambodian national university have all published these false titles.

The pattern exploits information asymmetry. A Philippines city mayor or a Cambodian provincial official has limited capacity to verify Singaporean academic appointments or British university degrees. When the Philippines DOST itself calls him "Dr.," who in that bureaucratic chain would question it? When JICA calls him "Adjunct Professor at NUS," who in Cambodia would know that NUS directories do not list him?

The title fraud is the foundation upon which other questionable claims rest. It is the single most objectively verifiable misconduct in this investigation — documented in primary sources across three countries, uncorrected for years, and directly facilitating access to government advisory roles that SCN's operations depend upon.
